Dear Editors,

I am submitting the manuscript ``The Smoothed-Check-Loss Trap: Why Convolution-Based
Corrections Fail in Quantile Regression with Measurement Error'' for consideration as an
original research paper in TEST.

The paper concerns quantile regression when the covariates are measured with classical
additive error, $W=X+U$. Corrected scores and conditional estimating equations are available
in fixed dimension, and penalised versions of them have been proposed for the case $p>n$. What
has not been settled is the shortcut that is common in applied work all the same: convolve the
check loss with the measurement error distribution and read the result as a corrected score.
The first half of the paper shows that this shortcut cannot work, and for the whole class of
kernels rather than for one kernel or one bandwidth. For any kernel with positive density at
zero the population score at the truth is $-(\Sigma_u\beta^*)d$; if the bandwidth is allowed to
depend on $\beta$ through any $M\succeq0$ the score becomes $(\Sigma_u+M)\beta^*d_M$, which for
the self-consistent choice $M=\Sigma_u$ is exactly twice the fixed-scale score. Under a
Gaussian design the smoothed loss is minimised exactly at the attenuated value
$\bar\beta=(\Sigma_x+\Sigma_u)^{-1}\Sigma_x\beta^*$, whatever the bandwidth, so the smoothed and
the naive estimator share their limit. The second half gives the repair. Conditioning on $W$
produces a calibrated regressor $\mu_W=CW$ under which an ordinary quantile regression is
correctly specified and its score is exactly mean-zero, so penalised estimation and debiased
inference become available. I give $\ell_1$ oracle inequalities in $p>n$, first for a known and
then for an estimated calibration; I prove that the debiased estimator is asymptotically normal
with the oracle-calibration variance under an explicit condition on the calibration; and I show
that the sample-covariance plug-in provably does not meet that condition, with the resulting
coverage loss measured in the simulations.

I believe the paper suits TEST. The contribution is theoretical, but each result is tied to a
practice: the negative half explains why a convenient implementation moves the estimand without
warning in the output, and the positive half is a methodology that a practitioner can run, with
the $\ell_1$ theory and the debiased intervals that go with it. The simulations and the
application to NHANES 2017--2018, where the measurement error covariance is estimated from two
24-hour dietary recalls rather than assumed, show the correction changing nutrient
coefficients severalfold. The scripts that produce every table and figure, together with their
saved output, are archived at \url{https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.23175827} (repository:
\url{https://github.com/CIKIXI/smoothed-check-loss-trap}).

The manuscript is original, is not under consideration elsewhere and has not been published
before. I am the sole author and I approve the submission. There is no funding to acknowledge
and no competing interests. All proofs are in the supplementary material.

Thank you for considering the paper.
