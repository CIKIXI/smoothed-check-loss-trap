Dear Editor,

I am submitting the manuscript ``The Smoothed-Check-Loss Trap: Why Convolution-Based
Corrections Fail in Quantile Regression with Measurement Error'' for consideration as a
research article in the \emph{Annals of Statistics}.

The paper concerns quantile regression when the covariates are measured with classical
additive error, $W=X+U$. Corrected scores and weighted corrections are available in fixed
dimension, and penalised versions of them have been proposed for the case $p>n$ that motivates
the literature. What has not been settled is the shortcut that is common in applied work all
the same: convolve the check loss with the measurement error distribution and read the result
as a corrected score. The first half of the paper shows that this shortcut cannot work, for the
whole class of kernels rather than for one kernel or one bandwidth. For any kernel with
positive density at zero the population score at the truth is $-(\Sigma_u\beta^*)d$; if the
bandwidth is allowed to depend on $\beta$ through any $M\succeq0$ the score becomes
$(\Sigma_u+M)\beta^*d_M$, which for the self-consistent choice $M=\Sigma_u$ is exactly twice
the fixed-scale score. Under the Gaussian design the fixed-scale smoothed loss is minimised
exactly at the attenuated value $\bar\beta=(\Sigma_x+\Sigma_u)^{-1}\Sigma_x\beta^*$, whatever
the bandwidth, so smoothing changes the finite-sample behaviour but not the parameter being
estimated. The smoothed and the naive estimator share that target.

The second half gives the repair and the theory that comes with it. Conditioning on $W$
(regression calibration) produces a regressor $\mu_W=CW$ such that
$Y=\mu_W^\top\beta^*+e$, with $e$ independent of $\mu_W$ and with a constant conditional
$\tau$-quantile, so an ordinary quantile regression of $Y$ on $(\mu_W,1)$ is correctly
specified and its score is exactly mean-zero. That is what makes debiased inference available
here and unavailable for the smoothed loss, whose score cannot be centred at any scale. I give
$\ell_1$ oracle inequalities in $p>n$, first for a known calibration and then for an estimated
one; the second case needs a perturbation bound for $\hat C$, a sup-norm bound for the score
and a transfer of the restricted eigenvalue condition, and I state that row-wise accuracy of
$\hat C$ is assumed rather than proved. I then prove that the debiased estimator is
asymptotically normal with the oracle-calibration variance, under an explicit condition on the
calibration: a known calibration meets it, the sample-covariance plug-in provably does not, and
the simulations measure the coverage that costs. The method is applied to NHANES 2017--2018,
where the measurement error covariance is estimated from two 24-hour dietary recalls rather
than assumed, and where the correction changes the nutrient coefficients several times over.

The two halves belong together: without the negative result the natural response to
measurement error in a quantile regression is to smooth, and the estimand moves with no warning
in the output. Readers working on measurement error, on high-dimensional quantile regression
and on debiased inference should each find something here.

The manuscript is original, is not under consideration elsewhere and has not been published
before. I am the sole author and I approve the submission. There is no funding to acknowledge
and no conflict of interest. All proofs are in the appendices, and every table and figure comes
from the saved output of the scripts listed in Appendix~C, which can be supplied as
supplementary material.

Thank you for considering the paper.
